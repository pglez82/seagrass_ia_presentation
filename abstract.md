## Artificial Intelligence Applied to the Mapping and Monitoring of Seagrass Meadows

Pablo González (AIC, Universidad de Oviedo)
Fernando García-González (IEO-CSIC)

Accurately determining the extent and health status of seagrass meadows is essential for the conservation of coastal ecosystems. However, monitoring these habitats typically requires expensive sampling campaigns and substantial manual analysis efforts. Artificial Intelligence is transforming this scenario, enabling the efficient exploitation of large volumes of data from multiple sources: satellites, drones, acoustic sensors, underwater imagery, etc.

In this talk, we will review the state of the art in Artificial Intelligence techniques applied to the mapping and monitoring of seagrass meadows. We will analyze the evolution from classical approaches, based on the independent classification of each observation (i.e. pixel-by-pixel classification), to modern deep learning methods capable of exploiting the spatial context present in the data to obtain much richer representations. Furthermore, we will introduce emerging lines of research based on foundation models and self-supervised learning, aimed at utilizing large amounts of unannotated data and reducing dependence on costly manual labeling processes.

Finally, we will focus on one of the main challenges in bringing these tools from the laboratory to operational applications: distribution shifts between the training set and the data presented to the model during inference (dataset shift). Unlike more controlled environments, marine ecosystems are inherently dynamic. Seagrass meadows experience seasonal cycles, degradation and recovery processes, structural changes associated with growth, and variations derived from environmental conditions. Apart from this, there are changes in water turbidity, lighting conditions, and the characteristics of the sensors used for data acquisition. Consequently, the main issue for the operational adoption of these technologies is not only improving model accuracy, but understanding and managing how data changes. Only by addressing this challenge will it be possible to develop reliable, transferable, and long-term sustainable monitoring systems.
